PROBLEM
-------
The explanatory power of a HWUT test is a property of the author, not
of the framework. A well painted picture localises a fault better than
any assert: three lines "alfred" / "caesar" / "berta" and the reader
sees at once that the second and third are swapped.

A poorly painted picture is a wall of repr() output with embedded
addresses, and it is notarised all the same.

Assert frameworks fail closed under a weak author: a bad assert is
still a named, scoped predicate. HWUT fails open. The best feature of
the tool is the one most dependent on the person holding it.

A linter cannot make a picture good. It can raise the floor.

CANDIDATE CHECKS
----------------
  - unstable-looking tokens in a GOOD file: hex addresses, absolute
    paths, timestamps, temporary directory names, version strings,
    process ids
  - no structure: single line, no labels, no separators
  - unlabelled numeric columns
  - GOOD file consisting of a boolean or of "OK"/"FAIL" only
    (the test asserts instead of displaying)
  - GOOD file whose content is dominated by one repr()-shaped run
  - GOOD file with no corresponding stimulus visible in the test
  - excessive width or length (a picture no one will read)
