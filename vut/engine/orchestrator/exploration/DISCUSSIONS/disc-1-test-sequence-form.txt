disc-1  A FORMAL FORM FOR THE TEST SEQUENCE                     ** OPEN **
______________________________________________________________________________

NOTED, NOT RULED. Written down so it is not lost; nothing here is built and
nothing here is decided.

THE THOUGHT

The test sequence is a RESULT, not a thing an author writes:

    test case specification  +  test configuration  ->  test sequence

and it may have a small grammar of its own -- a sequence of STEPS, each step
saying how its members stand to one another:

    parallel [ test0, test1 ]
    select   [ test1 -> test2 ]

Sketched, not settled. 'parallel' says these may run at once; 'select' names
an ordering between two. What the full set of step kinds is, and what an
arrow means exactly, is open.

WHY IT IS WORTH HAVING

A formal test sequence is something 'hwut.parse --test-sequence' can PRINT.
An author would then see not only what he configured (todo-7) but what the
framework intends to DO with it -- the schedule itself, before anything
runs.

WHERE IT MAY LEAD

The note is kept because it looks like it may help considerably later. A
sequence with a form of its own is a thing that can be printed, diffed,
recorded, replayed, and reasoned about -- and a scheduler that emits one is
answerable for its decisions in a way that a scheduler emitting only
behaviour is not.

WHAT IT STANDS ON

Exploration already settles the inputs: 'collision' says what may not run at
the same time, 'dependency' says what must have run first, and [MISDEP] says
what cannot be reached at all (R-32, R-33, R-35, R-36). The sequence is what
a scheduler makes of those three, and the scheduler is the orchestrator's.

OPEN, all of it:
  -  the step kinds and their grammar
  -  whether the form is written down anywhere or exists only in flight
  -  who owns it: exploration produces the relations, the orchestrator
     produces the sequence -- so the form is probably the orchestrator's
     and this note belongs there once that component is opened
  -  what '--test-sequence' prints when the graph carries a cycle
