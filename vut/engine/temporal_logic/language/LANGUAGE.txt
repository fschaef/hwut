==============================================================================
LANGUAGE
==============================================================================

The rule-file language describes the behaviour of an entity as reactions to
events. This document states each language element and shows it in an example.
Rationale for every decision lives in RATIONALE.txt (entries R-1..R-28); this
document carries mechanics only.

Spine:

                                 character
                                     |
            .------------------------+-------------------.
            |                        |                   |
         aspect                   aspect      ...      aspect
            |                        |                   |
      .-----+-----.             .----+----.          .--+--.
   behavior   behavior       behavior  behavior   behavior  ...
      |           |             |          |          |
  causality   causality     causality  causality  causality

Real change happens at the causality level. Behaviour aggregates causalities;
an aspect governs which one behaviour is active; a character circumscribes all
aspects.


(0) Foundations: two natures, four relations, one law
======================================================
Everything the language speaks of has one of TWO NATURES, and every
construct in this document is an instance of the relations below. This
section is the design's spine; where mechanics await the type document
they are marked, but the model here governs regardless.

(0.1) The two natures
---------------------
PERSISTENT OBJECTS exist over time and occupy resources; their existence
is a RESPONSIBILITY. They come into being by construction and leave it by
destruction -- both are WORKS (12): construction hands a new debt to the
caller through a gives-port; destruction receives a debt through a
takes-port and ends it. Composing already-answered-for parts into a
record is AGGREGATION, not work: it creates no new debt beyond its parts.

EPHEMERALS -- events, and the signals built on them -- are defined on the
fly and VAPORATE UPON CONSUMPTION. They may be known or taken, never had;
consumption is their end -- no destruction, no giving onward, no ledger.
Ephemerals are the language's channel of COMMUNICATION, including the
communication of MISBEHAVIOUR: a signal is an ephemeral reporting
malfunction (12.2), consumed by the handler arm that matches it.

The two natures obey different laws throughout; a rule stated for one
never silently applies to the other.

(0.2) The four relations
------------------------
One verb, one relation, everywhere -- member declarations, panels, and
transfers all speak the same four words:

    is:      IDENTITY.  What a thing is; inheritance. Creates no
             possession and no debt.
    has:     CUSTODY.   Aggregation of persistent objects. To have is to
             owe: the haver answers for the end of what it has.
    knows:   ACQUAINTANCE.  Access without responsibility -- a view.
             Knowing creates no debt and confers no destruction rights;
             the known must outlive the knower's use. Only ephemerals
             and knowledge are ever merely known.
    give     TRANSFER.  Custody moves WHOLE: the giver's name dies, the
             taker now has. Giving is moving, never copying -- one
             object, one debt. (takes:/gives: are the panel spellings of
             receiving and handing custody, 12.2.)

(0.3) The law of ownership tracing
----------------------------------
    GIVE OR DESTRUCT WHAT YOU HAVE.

On every path, every persistent object that is had is either GIVEN onward
or DESTRUCTED by its haver before that haver's own end. Abandonment is
the one crime. The trace of responsibility runs unbroken from the
construction site to the destruction site, through every give between --
statically checkable, because every step is one of the four relations
written in the source. (The ledger's mechanics -- the checker, containers
as collective debt, disposal policies -- bind the future type document;
the law binds now.)

(0.4) The line of thought
-------------------------
The language builds outward from this section, in this order:

    natures and relations        (this section)
    the class concept            characters and types: is/has/knows members
    the work                     the act: panels speak the same verbs;
                                 construction and destruction are works (12)
    the clockwork                the temporal work (11)
    connections                  pull, signals, pipelines (11.1)

Persistent objects and ephemerals remain of different nature at every
stage; whenever a construct touches both, its law says which nature each
part addresses.


(1) Lexical laws
================

(1.1) Keywords end with a colon
-------------------------------
Every keyword is colon-terminated; the colon is part of the keyword.

    is:  has:  when:  knows:  signals:
    if:  elif:  else:  match:  case:  for:  int:  float:  step:  from:  start:
    break:  continue:  dropto:  every:  as:  in:  with:
    open:  import:

Exception -- operator, type, and definition-kind words are BARE:

    and  or  not  in  true  false  struct  list  dict
    character  aspect  behavior  cause

"in" is therefore a taken word: no identifier may be named "in"; the four
definition-kind words are likewise taken (they follow a definition's colon,
see 1.2).

RESERVED -- specified in (11) and (12), implementation pending (R-24..
R-27): the keywords "exit:", "groove:", "tick:", "ticks:", "takes:",
"gives:", "finish:", "elsewhile:", "elseto:", the bare words "finish"
(the jump to the finish: terminal), "finished" (the built-in exhaustion
variant), "give", and the bare kind words "work", "clockwork".

(1.2) Definitions are name-first
--------------------------------
Every definition follows ONE form -- the name leads, the kind follows the
colon, the parameter list rides the kind word:

    <name> : [~] <kind>[(<parameters>)] <kind sections>

    Polling : behavior { ... }
    hot : cause(threshold) temp_high when: e.value > threshold;
    GENERAL : ~behavior { ... }

Read: "<name> is a <kind> about ...". "~" qualifies the kind word (abstract,
see 3/6/7). The form is shared with the member declaration "name: type;" --
one head, and the word after the colon decides: a definition-kind word opens
a definition, a type ends a top-level declaration.

(1.3) Docstrings
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
A DOCSTRING is a triple-quoted string -- three double-quotes on each side --
and may span lines. It PRECEDES what it documents:

    """Watches the motor temperature and shuts down on overheat."""
    guard : behavior(threshold = 90.0) {
        hot(threshold) => shutdown;
    }

A docstring may document a character, an aspect, a behaviour, or a named
cause. A docstring that precedes no definition is the MODULE docstring and
stands as the very first item of the file -- anywhere else it is an error.
Docstrings carry documentation only: they take no part in execution and do
not enter the export surface.

(1.4) Blocks are brace-delimited
--------------------------------
Every block is "{ … }"; braces are mandatory at every depth. A colon-keyword
introduces; a brace-pair delimits.

    if: <condition> { … }

(1.5) Comments
--------------
A comment runs from "#" to end of line. "#" is the sole comment delimiter.

    c.count += 1;   # advance the counter


(2) Causality (atomic behaviour)
================================

A causality links a cause to one or more effects: cause --> effects. It is
anchored in an event — an ephemeral entity of receivable change.

    button_pressed => light_on;

GRAMMAR
    "<causality>": ("<cause>", "<effects>")

(2.1) Cause
-----------
A cause is an event with an optional guard. The guard is a CONDITION (see 4)
introduced by "when:". The cause is the SOLE place a guard attaches.

    MOTOR_ON when: c.fuel == 0 => lighten_fuel_missing;

GRAMMAR
    "<cause-explicit>": ("<event>", ["when:", "<condition>"])

A cause may be named, parameterised, and referenced. The default parameter type
is float. A parameter may carry a VALUE DEFAULT (Python behaviour):

    <name> [: <type>] [= <algebraic>]

A use supplies every parameter WITHOUT a default, each at most once,
positionals before named arguments; a named argument binds a declared
parameter by name with the port binder: "name => value" (D-24; "=" is the
declaration default marker only). A BARE name is a reference, not a call -- no argument judgement
attaches. A named cause CONTAINS its guard; a use site supplies arguments,
not a second guard.

    OPERATION_IMPOSSIBLE : cause(fuel_limit, battery_limit)
        MOTOR_ON when: c.fuel <= fuel_limit or c.battery < battery_limit;

    OPERATION_IMPOSSIBLE(0.1, 2) => SIGNAL_REFUSE_OPERATION;

GRAMMAR
    "<cause-tail>": (["<parens-decl>"], "<cause-explicit>", ";")
    "<cause>":      ("<cause-explicit>", OR, "<call>")

(A named cause is defined through the one name-first form of 1.4:
"<name> : cause(<parameters>) <cause-explicit> ;".)

(2.2) Effects
-------------
An effect is a marker followed by a target or a command-block.

    "=>"    activate
    "=x=>"  deactivate

GRAMMAR
    "<effects>":       ("<effect-marker>",
                        (("<code(causality)>", ["<effects>"]),
                         OR,
                         ("<spawn>", (";", OR, "<effects>"))))
    "<effect-marker>": ("=>", OR, "=x=>")

TERMINATOR LAW: ";" terminates a statement or effect; "}" terminates itself
-- never "};". The chain is right-recursive: the FINAL effect decides -- a
block-final causality ends at its "}", a spawn-final causality takes ";".

    E => go => stop;            spawn-final: ";"
    E => { c.n += 1; }          block-final: ends at "}"
    E => { c.n += 1; } => go;   block mid-chain, spawn-final: ";"

"=x=> X" is well-formed iff X is deactivable:

    entity kind          deactivable
    -------------------  -----------
    event                no
    command-block        no
    behavior             yes
    aspect               yes
    character            yes
    recurring-emission   yes        (the "as:" handle; see 2.4)

"<call>" and "<code>" are disjoint: a command-block contains statements only; an
entity target is a "<call>". An effect needing both a mutation and an entity
target is written as two effects on one cause.

(2.3) Reference and definition
------------------------------
The declaration side is the name-first head of 1.4: the defined name, then
":", then the kind word carrying the formal parameters -- one shared form
across cause, behaviour, aspect, and character (the AST reunites head name
and parameters into one signature product). "<call>" is the use side (name +
arguments). A "(role)" suffix on a reference — e.g.
"<call(behavior)>" — is an advisory role hint walked by position; it is
non-identity and resolved in the semantic pass.

(2.4) Recurring emission
------------------------
An ordinary "=>" spawn becomes recurring with "every:"; an optional "as:" binds
a cancellation handle.

    => <call(event)> every: <numeric> [as: <name>]

"every: <numeric>" emits the event once per <numeric> (seconds by convention).
"as: <name>" names the emission so it can be cancelled with "=x=> <name>". The
handle is a bare name scoped to the enclosing entity. A recurring emission runs
only while its entity is active and is cancelled automatically at ~EXIT. "as:"
requires "every:".

    Polling : behavior {
        ~ENTRY       => poll_sensors() every: 0.5 as: poller;
        stop_pressed =x=> poller;
    }


(3) Behaviour
=============

A behaviour aggregates causalities: what happens, for a set of events, while
that behaviour is active.

    ClimateControlWhenActivated : behavior {
        ac_button_pushed => toggle_compressor;
        temp_dial_turned => set_target_temperature;
        fan_dial_turned  => adjust_fan_speed;
    }

GRAMMAR
    "<behavior-def>":  (t_ID("name"), ":", ["~"], "behavior", "<behavior-tail>")
    "<behavior-tail>": (["<parens-decl>"],
                        ["is:", "<call(behavior)>", STAR((",", "<call(behavior)>"))],
                        ["has:", "<declaration-list>"],
                        "{", PLUS("<causality>"), "}")

The body opens directly with "{" (R-12). "is:" (inheritance) and "has:"
(declarations) are optional sections before it. ": ~behavior" is abstract — it never instantiates
alone and exists to be inherited.

"~ENTRY" and "~EXIT" are reserved, non-externally-triggered events that run on
activation and deactivation. They take no external trigger and no guard, and
their effect is any causality effect (a spawn or a command-block, see 2/5) --
e.g. a recurring "every:" spawn at ~ENTRY, auto-cancelled at ~EXIT (R-15). The
signature parameters are available through the "s." binding during ~ENTRY/~EXIT
and in "has:".


(4) Values: three sorts, two operator worlds
============================================

Values come in three sorts: a number, a truth, and a collection. The number and
truth sorts are operator worlds connected by exactly two bridges. The collection
sort has no operators -- it is constructed and consumed only. Mutation (see 5) is
a separate thing that yields no value.

ALGEBRAIC — produces a number.
    operators  + - * /        grouping ( )
    primary    numeric literal | data-access | ternary

CONDITION — produces a truth.
    operators  and or not      grouping ( )
    primary    bare condition-valued data-access | comparison | true | false

COLLECTION — a value sort with no arithmetic operators; constructed by a
    comprehension (see 4.3) and consumed by for:, an index base, a
    comprehension from:, an argument, or a mutation right-hand side.
    Compared for equality (STRUCTURAL) and searched with "in".

STRING — a quoted literal ("…") or a string member; no operator world of its
    own. Compared with == / != and searched with "in" (substring). A string
    literal is an expression atom.

BRIDGES (the only crossings into truth)
    comparison   algebraic {== != < <= > >=} algebraic -> condition
                 chaining allowed:  a < b < c  ==  a < b and b < c
    equality     two like values {== !=} -> condition; collections and
                 strings compare STRUCTURALLY (element-wise, character-wise)
    membership   <value> {in | not in} <collection or string> -> condition
                 element of a list, KEY of a dict, substring of a string
    ternary      condition ? algebraic : algebraic -> algebraic

A guard ("when:"), an "if:"/"elif:" test, and a "match:" scrutinee comparison
consume a CONDITION. Arguments and loop bounds consume an ALGEBRAIC value. A
mutation right-hand side is any sort.

(4.1) Bindings
--------------
Every member access names its binding; there is no leading-dot form.

    e.<name>   member of the event that triggered the causality
    b.<name>   member of the behaviour instance     (behaviour, causality)
    a.<name>   member of the aspect instance         (aspect, behaviour, causality)
    c.<name>   member of the character instance       (always)
    s.<name>   argument passed to the entity's call    (everywhere in the entity)

(4.2) Data-access
-----------------
Data-access reaches a value out of a structure: a binding-qualified name or a
call, followed by zero or more index steps. It is the place layer the value
worlds, the mutation lvalue, the for: source, and the comprehension from:
all draw on. An lvalue is a data-access, so an indexed place is assignable.

    c.fuel              base only
    c.buf[i]            one index step
    grid[i][j]          chained steps (multi-dimension)
    f(x)[0]             call result, indexed
    c.buf[i] = v ;      data-access as an assignment target

Single index only; the index is an algebraic value; multi-dimension is chaining.

(4.3) Comprehension
-------------------
A comprehension constructs a collection from one or more generators with optional
filters. Generators chain left-to-right; each may carry an "if:" filter. Loop
variables are bare locals scoped to the comprehension, and may destructure.

    [ x*y   with: x, y from: pairs ]
    [ x     with: x from: xs if: x > 0 ]
    [ x + y with: x from: xs if: x > 0
            with: y from: ys ]
    [ [ a*b with: b from: row ] with: row from: matrix ]   nested

The element and each source may be any sort; a collection element nests.


(5) Command-block (imperative body)
===================================

A command-block is the "<code>" effect: it runs imperative statements and
mutates hidden variables. It runs immediately and uninterrupted when its cause
fires, is one atomic terminal leaf (no fan-out, no interleaving), and emits no
events at any depth.

    TRAVELLED_100KM => {
        c.fuel -= 8.31;
        if: c.fuel < 5.0   { c.warn = true; }
        elif: c.fuel < 20.0 { c.warn = false; }
        else:               { c.reserve = false; }
        int: i = 1 .. c.cylinders { c.checks += i; }
    }

Statements:

    mutation        <lvalue> {= += -= *= /=} <algebraic> ;
                    lvalue is a binding-qualified member, head in {e. b. a. c.};
                    "s." is the read-only inbound argument, never a target.

    if:/elif:/else: if: <condition> { … } [elif: <condition> { … }]* [else: { … }]
                    a statement brancher (NOT the value-world ternary).

    match:/case:    match: <scrutinee> { case: <pattern> { … } ... [case: _ { … }] }
                    scrutinee is an algebraic value; patterns are literal, range,
                    or glob (string-wildcard); optional default "case: _".
                    Unmatched with no default is a no-op.

    for:            for: <var> in: <collection> { … }
                    binds <var> to each element (element only, no index).

    count loop      <count-type>: <var> = <algebraic> .. <algebraic> [step: <algebraic>] { … }
                    declares a typed counter <var> inline; <count-type> is
                    int: or float: (extensible). Inclusive bounds; ".." is the
                    range operator; default step +1; "step:" sets it (negative
                    counts down). The counter is a bare local.

    enumeration     <count-type>: <i> with: <x> from: <iterable> [start: <i0>] { … }
                    the count loop's enumeration arm: <i> counts in the
                    counter's type from <i0> (default 0) while <x> walks the
                    iterable -- index and element, paired. Both are bare
                    locals.

A loop variable (for:'s var, the count loop's typed counter, the
enumeration's index and element) is a bare local name, scoped to its loop
body, never escaping it.

(5.1) Loop control
------------------
    break:      exits the nearest enclosing loop (unlabelled).
    continue:   next iteration of the nearest enclosing loop.

(5.2) Exit regions
------------------
The outermost command-block body may end in a tail ladder of bare EXIT-LABELS.
":<label>:" DEFINES a label (no body, C-label drop-through: control reaching
it falls through to what follows). The label is SELF-TERMINATING: the second
colon delimits it, as "}" delimits a block -- no ";" follows (R-24).
"dropto: <label>" is the forward-only JUMP to such an exit. This is the
kernel "goto out:" cleanup idiom made safe -- "goto considered harmful"
(Dijkstra) answered by allowing only forward drops to a declared exit.

    SOMETHING => {
        c.h = acquire_a();
        if: not c.h.ok  { dropto: fail; }
        c.g = acquire_b();
        if: not c.g.ok  { dropto: free_a; }
        dropto: done;

      :free_a:   c.released_a = release_a(c.h);
      :fail:     c.status = FAILED;
      :done:
    }

"dropto:" requires a target and may jump only to an exit-label defined LATER in
the body (forward-only, so it forms no loop). Exit-labels may be defined only at
the outermost body -- never inside a loop, if/elif/else, match, or any nested
block. Loops use the unlabelled "break:"/"continue:"; exit-regions use "dropto:"
(jump) and ":<label>:" (define); the two never overlap. The keyword "exit:"
carries no duty in this language version: it is reserved (R-24).


(6) Aspect
==========

An aspect governs the one behaviour active at a given time; its behaviours are
mutually exclusive.

    MotorActivity : aspect {
        GENERAL : ~behavior {
             gas_pedal_release => close_throttle;
             battery_empty     => close_throttle;
        }
        ON : behavior is: GENERAL {
             gas_pedal_push     => open_throttle;
             on_off_button_push => OFF;
        }
        OFF : behavior is: GENERAL {
             gas_pedal_push     => tone_notify_engine_off;
             on_off_button_push => ON;
        }
    }

GRAMMAR
    "<aspect-tail>": (["<panel>"],
                 ["is:", "<call(aspect)>", STAR((",", "<call(aspect)>"))],
                 ["has:", "<declaration-list>"],
                 "{", PLUS("<behavior-def>"), "}")
    "<panel>":       ("(", ["knows:", <decl-args>], ["signals:", <signal-decls>], ")")
    "<signal-decl>": (name, ["<parens-decl>"])

": ~aspect" is abstract. "is:" inherits. "~ENTRY"/"~EXIT" work as for behaviour.

THE PANEL (D-23). An aspect's kind parens are a sectioned signature, the
clockwork-style form: "knows:" declares the parameters (what the aspect is
told at instantiation -- the former plain parameter list), "signals:" the
declared fault set. Sections are keyword-led and juxtaposed without a
separator; entries within a section are comma-separated; both sections are
optional.

    gauge : aspect(knows: rate = 10 signals: overrun, sensor_lost(port)) {
        poll : behavior { sample => publish; }
    }

Signals are RECORDED on the definition; their checks and their consumption
(pipes wired at the winding site) land with the work construct.


(7) Character
=============

A character aggregates aspects, which are concurrent: each aspect is
independently activable (active/inactive), and two or more may co-exist. Aspects
appear in the "has:" section.

    sprite : ~character has: {
        position:  2dvector;
        speed:     2dvector;
        resources: AspectResources(0, 0, 0);
    }

GRAMMAR
    "<character-tail>": (["<parens-decl>"],
                    ["is:", "<call(character)>", STAR((",", "<call(character)>"))],
                    ["has:", "<declaration-list>"])

Composition has two layers, not two peer kinds: within an aspect, behaviours are
mutually exclusive (the aspect governs which one is active); within a character,
aspects are concurrent and each is independently activable.


(8) Namespaces and imports
==========================

A namespace is a named scope holding nested declarations. It is re-openable and
nests to any depth; names declared within are scoped to it.

    open: graphics.sprites {
        sprite : ~character has: { position: 2dvector; }
        ...
    }

An import mounts a file or url under a namespace. The path is a quoted string.

    import: "lib/physics.vut" as: physics
    import: "https://example.org/shapes.vut" as: shapes

A namespace path (graphics.sprites, physics) is a plain dotted name -- distinct
from a binding-qualified member access (4.1), which always has an
e./b./a./c./s. head. The parser records an import; the semantic pass mounts it
and resolves names scope-aware, walking outward from the current scope.


(9) Relation to other terminology
=================================

This section is the single place that relates the native spine to traditional
statechart vocabulary. These terms are NOT used elsewhere in the language.

    native       statechart
    -----------  ------------------------------------------------
    causality    transition / reaction
    behavior     simple state
    aspect       state machine / composite state, governing XOR
    character    hierarchical composite state, AND / orthogonal

AND-composition is modelled as a state machine per state (active/inactive), not
as a peer of XOR: exclusivity lives inside an aspect; independent activation
lives above it, at the character.


(10) Aggregation types
======================

Declarations admit three plain VARIABLE-TYPED containers (no type parameters,
no member functions):

    buffer:  list;                 a sequence of anything
    scores:  dict;                 a map of anything to anything
    point:   struct { x; y; };     named slots

A type is a built-in (int float string bool), one of these containers, or a
named type. The containers are untyped for now; type constraints (a typed list,
dict, or struct field) are a later addition.


(11) Clockwork              [SETTLED -- implementation lands with the work construct]
===============================================================================
A CLOCKWORK is the work family's temporal member: a pull-driven producer,
the single linearization of a state machine (kin to the aspect, whose
behaviours are free states; the kinship is noted, the constructs are
separate, R-25).

(11.1) Topology
---------------
A clockwork may PULL MANY (its inputs are themselves clockworks, consumed
via next()), may KNOW things, and is PULLED BY EXACTLY ONE consumer through
its single outward port, next(). Its signals are received by the puller's
"else:" handler. The winding relation admits no cycle. Pipelines are chains
of clockworks in which only the final consumer pulls; each stage's
exhaustion and faults translate upward through ordinary handlers -- forced
translation, flowing through time.

(11.2) Panel
------------
The definition follows the one form (1.2) with the panel verbs:

    knows:    inputs known for the run -- no responsibility taken
    takes:    inputs received into custody at winding
    ticks:    the per-tick outputs (in place of gives: -- panel and
              terminal correspond: ticks: declares, tick: delivers)
    signals:  the closed fault set

    line_reader : clockwork(takes: f : file_handle
                            ticks: line : string
                            signals: io_error(code: int)) { ... }

What a clockwork takes at winding it holds across ticks. (The custody
ledger across ticks and the written early-disposal path "winddown:" bind
the future type document.)

(11.3) Egresses and waits
-------------------------
Three egresses close a body stretch:

    tick:             deliver the declared ticks: bundle implicitly (as
                      finish: does for gives:) and SUSPEND until the next
                      pull; no operands -- the panel already named
                      everything
    finish:           orderly exhaustion; the unique success terminal
    exit: <variant>   fault mid-stream, one of the own declared signals

Three waits suspend a running clockwork, one law for all -- progress is
guaranteed because each is crossed only when the outside world acts:

    tick:             awaits the next pull from below
    groove: { arms }  awaits causes (the clockwork's WAIT construct,
                      R-25(3)): the script parks; a firing arm cause runs
                      its arm and the script continues -- an arm may
                      deliver (tick:) or fault (exit:) from within
    next() on input   awaits delivery from above; EVERY next() is a
                      suspension point of the caller

(11.4) The pull
---------------
next() is the event that runs the clockwork: the puller is the clock.
There is ONE spelling -- next() is intrinsically potentially-suspending;
no wait-variant exists. Its match domain is the declared signals: set
UNION the built-in variant "finished" (exhaustion is an event -- no
option types, no sentinels):

    v = cd.next() else: {
        finished => break:;
    }

Winding is an ordinary call -- no constructor: instantiation is calling
the clockwork's name with construction arguments, an aware assignment
with handled faults.

(11.5) Consumption loop
-----------------------
    for: v from: cd { ... } else: { ... }

"from:" announces the pulled source as "elsewhile:" announces its
backward edge; "finished" IS the loop's own end. The trailing else: must
cover the remaining signal set and is omitted exactly when that set is
empty. Custody flavours ("from: cd" borrows -- disposal stays with the
consumer; "from: give cd" hands the instance to the loop, which disposes
on every exit path) bind the future type document.

(11.6) A complete small clockwork
---------------------------------
    countdown : clockwork(knows: n : int
                          ticks: value : int) {
        int: i = 1 .. n {
            value = n - i + 1;
            tick:
        }
        finish:
    }

    for: v from: countdown(10) { print(v); }


(12) Work                   [SETTLED -- implementation lands in the F-9 passes]
===============================================================================
A WORK replaces the function/exception pair: a subroutine with named input
and output PORTS (as an entity in VHDL) and a mandatory, non-reifiable
FAULT CHANNEL. Everything that can leave a work -- outputs and malfunction
signals alike -- stands on its face; the call site is statically obligated
to handle every declared signal. Invoking a work is a CALL.

(12.1) Definition forms
-----------------------
The one form of 1.2 conquers all three:

    <name> : work( <panel> ) { <body> }     a defined work
    <name> : work( <panel> )                a SPEC (panel without machine)
    <name> : <spec-name> { <body> }         an IMPLEMENTATION of that spec

(12.2) The panel
----------------
Panel verbs -- the relations of (0.2) spoken at a work's boundary
(entries comma-separated within a section; sections keyword-led
and juxtaposed; each entry "<name> [: <type>] [= <default>]"; "=" is the
declaration DEFAULT marker -- the call-site binder is "=>", 12.5):

    knows:    inputs known for the call, no responsibility taken; a
              defaulted knows-entry may be omitted at the call site;
              defaulted entries follow non-defaulted ones
    takes:    inputs received into custody (never defaulted -- a default
              would conjure custody from nowhere); custody law binds the
              future type document
    gives:    the outputs -- ordinary local variables of the body,
              implicitly given at finish:; a default initializer counts
              as assigned from the start
    signals:  the closed fault set: each signal an on-the-fly enumeration
              variant, optionally carrying named typed payload fields --
              "no_convergence(residual: float)". A work without signals:
              cannot signal; its call sites carry no handling obligation.

    bundle_adjustment : work(knows: pc_a : cloud, pc_b : cloud,
                                    eps : float = 1e-6
                             gives: center : point2d, rotation : mat2
                             signals: no_convergence(residual: float),
                                      singular_matrix(rank: int)) { ... }

SCOPE OF SIGNALS. A work's variants exist in exactly four places: its
declaration, its body (as exit: targets), the else:-handlers at its call
sites, and work specs (12.8). They are not types in the surrounding
program. Two works may both declare "no_convergence"; these are unrelated
and can never meet -- except measured against one spec, whose vocabulary
then governs.

(12.3) Result semantics
-----------------------
The result of a call is a SUM: either the complete bundle of all declared
outputs, or exactly one signal. Never both, never neither, never a partial
output set. After a successfully handled call, all bound outputs are
unconditionally valid.

(12.4) Body terminals
---------------------
    finish:                    the UNIQUE success terminal, exactly once;
                               required iff gives-entries are declared.
                               Every path reaching it must have every
                               gives-entry definitely assigned; the
                               declared giving happens implicitly, no
                               operands. Bare "finish" elsewhere jumps to
                               it, verified the same way.
    exit: <variant>(<payload>) the fault egress -- anywhere, any number of
                               times; leaves the work immediately with
                               that one own signal; no outputs. Payload
                               expressions evaluate at the exit:.

Both directions are checked: every exit: names a declared variant; every
self-declared variant is emitted by at least one reachable exit:.

(12.5) Call-site binding
------------------------
INPUTS follow the Python argument pattern: positionals bind in declaration
order; named arguments "port => value" bind by name; positionals precede
named; no port bound twice; every non-defaulted input supplied.

OUTPUTS bind in one of two forms:
  - assignment form: targets left of "=" bind gives-entries in
    declaration order -- "c, r = bundle_adjustment(pc1, pc2) else: ...";
  - port-map form: inputs and outputs associated by name in ONE
    parenthesis; argument/result dissolves into port DIRECTION and the
    call reads as a wiring diagram:
    "bundle_adjustment(pc_a => pc1, pc_b => pc2, center => c, rotation => r) else: ..."

SAME-TYPE RULE (outputs only): positional output binding is refused when
two gives-entries share a type; the port-map form is demanded.

VISIBLE DISCARD: every gives-entry is bound or visibly discarded -- "_"
positionally ("c, _ = ..."), or a trailing "_" in the port-map form for
"the rest is deliberately dropped". A call binding neither all ports nor
a discard does not compile.

(12.6) The aware assignment and the handler
-------------------------------------------
    c, r = bundle_adjustment(pc1, pc2) else: {
        no_convergence(res) => { log(res); exit: adjustment_failed(res); }
        singular_matrix(_)  => exit: bad_input;
    }

A call of a signalling work MUST carry an else: block (a work without
signals takes none). No signal object is bound -- "else: {", never
"else: e {": the fault channel selects an arm; it is not a value.

The else: block is a match over the signal set:
  - arms carry variant patterns with payload destructuring; a bare "=>"
    is the default arm (binds nothing, at most one, last);
  - FIRST-MATCH precedence; a DEAD ARM is a hard error;
  - EXHAUSTIVENESS over the closed set is mandatory;
  - the minimal legal handler is the EMPTY BLOCK "else: {}" -- the written
    shrug; silence does not compile, deliberate omission leaves a
    searchable trace (legality constrained by the flow rule below);
  - PAYLOAD SCOPING: a variant's fields materialize only inside the arm
    that matched; the default arm can react but cannot inspect.

WHAT AN ARM MUST DO -- each arm independently:
  1. DIVERGE ("finish", "exit: <signal>", or a provably non-falling path);
  2. PRODUCE SUBSTITUTES (assign the targets; ordinary definite
     assignment); or
  3. FALL THROUGH -- legal only where no invalid binding remains:
     statement-form call, or every target pre-initialized (keeps its
     prior value). Fresh bindings with a falling-through arm do not
     compile.
Code after an aware assignment sees fully valid outputs, unconditionally.

(12.7) The handler family: elseto: and elsewhile:, and the common ground
------------------------------------------------------------------------
First morpheme names the channel, second the control flow: "else:" handle
once; "elsewhile:" handle while it faults; "elseto:" handle over there.

ROUTED HANDLING. "elseto: <label> with: <carrier>;" routes this site's
fault wire to a label region further DOWN in the same body -- the honest
form of the kernel unwind cascade:

    f   = open_file(path)    else: { no_access(p) => exit: unreadable(p); }
    buf = alloc(65536)       elseto: close_f with: otto;
    n   = read_into(f, buf)  elseto: free_b  with: karl;
    ...
    finish:

    :free_b: {
        karl.short_read(got) => log(got);
        karl.=>              => ;
    }
        free(buf);
    :close_f:
        close(f);
    exit: unreadable("io");

  - labels are spelled ":name:" (5.2); a LABEL REGION is the label, an
    OPTIONAL match block, and statements; regions stack into the FAULT
    SECTION;
  - CARRIERS are labels, not values: "with: otto" names the arrival
    channel; legal only as a pattern qualifier "otto.<variant>(...)" in
    the label's match block; carriers are inferred from routes;
  - match blocks are ARRIVAL-SPECIFIC, statements are SHARED: control
    cascading down from above skips the block; a label with no match
    block is the routed omission marker;
  - exhaustiveness PER CARRIER when a block is present; "otto.=>" the
    per-carrier default, a final bare "=>" the label-wide default;
  - every path through the fault section ends deliberately: an
    "exit: <own signal>" or a jump to "finish" (legal only with all
    gives-entries assigned -- a recovered fault); no fall-off, no fall-IN
    from the normal path;
  - DOWNWARD ONLY, SAME BODY ONLY; definite assignment holds on every
    route and cascade path.

COMMON GROUND. An inline block may end in a ground opened by the divider
"==": every non-diverging arm drops in; the flow obligation of 12.6 moves
to the ground's end; payloads are dead below "=="; at most one "==",
after all arms:

    x, y = some_work(a, b) elsewhile: {
        no_convergence(r) => { eps = eps * 10; }
        hard_error(m)     => exit: broken(m);
        ==
        attempts = attempts + 1;
        if: attempts >= 5 { exit: gave_up(attempts) }
    }

LOOPING CALL. "elsewhile:" re-executes the aware assignment at the
block's end (arguments re-evaluated afresh); on success, outputs bind and
control continues after the block. "break:" leaves without success,
legal under the fall-through conditions of 12.6. "elsewhile: {}" is the
visible retry-until-success loop. Inline form only: routed regions stay
loop-free.

(12.8) Specs and work references
--------------------------------
A SPEC is the panel without the machine (12.1). A work conforms by
SPEC-TYPED DECLARATION ("name : spec_name { }": the spec supplies ports
and signals wholesale) or by STRUCTURAL fit (ports match by direction,
position, type; own signals a SUBSET of the spec's). A WORK REFERENCE is
a value denoting a work answering to a spec; the spec name is its type --
variables, ports, containers (dispatch tables). An indirect call is
checked against the SPEC: handler mandatory and exhaustive over the
spec's set; the spec's names govern; specs declare no input defaults. A
spec-typed member need not emit every inherited variant (the spec is a
promise for a family); the every-variant-emittable check of 12.4 applies
to self-declared sets only.

(12.9) Fallible primitives
--------------------------
Division and modulo may emit the built-in variant "div_by_zero"; a
statement containing a fallible division carries a handler -- the same
three forms. One handler per statement, over the UNION of the statement's
fault sources; evaluation is left-to-right. Provably nonzero divisors are
exempt (SEMANTICS 21 holds this check today). "div_by_zero" obeys every
ordinary signal rule. The principle generalizes to any fallible primitive
the language later defines.

(12.10) Signals never travel
----------------------------
A variant is not nameable outside the four places of 12.2, so re-raising
a foreign signal is INEXPRESSIBLE: a work reporting trouble upward exits
with one of its OWN variants -- every level speaks of malfunction in its
own vocabulary. Payloads may travel; tags may not. A signal can never be
stored, collected, or re-raised at a distance: a branch taken, not a
value.

(12.11) Events on the data side
-------------------------------
The EVENT of section 2 and the mechanism behind signals: are ONE concept
-- an ephemeral value. An event-typed knows-/takes-entry declares the
variants that travel with the call ("knows: level : event(GRADE_5,
FULL(v: int))"); the bare names are in scope exactly within the argument
list. The EVENT MATCHER applies the handler-block anatomy to an event
variable, all rules of 12.6 unchanged. Events may be known or taken,
never had -- ephemeral is a law, not a mood (custody wording binds the
future type document).
