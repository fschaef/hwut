HAVE, KNOW, BE -- the lifecycle document                       (R-40, F-9)
==============================================================================
Authority: LANGUAGE.txt carries the LAW (0.1-0.5, 10, 11); this document
carries the lifecycle STORY -- how the three relations govern an object
from construction to destruction, and the runtime model the emitter owes.
Settled by R-28..R-40; open forks live in OPEN.txt.

(1) BE -- identity
------------------
"is" is the relation of identity: a rule's product IS-A its product type
(A-11); a class IS what its is: line says, transitively. Being is not a
lifecycle event -- it is true for the whole life, from the constructor's
give: to the disposal work's last statement. Identity never dangles: no
object outlives its being.

(2) HAVE -- custody and debt
----------------------------
Having is EXISTENCE-BEARING: the had thing exists, always (0.5 -- Nothing
is never had). Custody begins at a constructor's gives-port, moves WHOLE
along give-steps (one object, one debt, one haver at a time), and ends in
exactly one of two ways: GIVEN onward, or DESTRUCTED (0.3). A local
variable is had by the work that has it: the terminal (give:/exit:) ends
the having.

THE GIVE LEAVES NOTHING BEHIND. Upon give, the giver's have-relationship
becomes HAVE NOTHING: the name is dead. An immediate 'know that' -- any
attempt to keep an acquaintance with the given-away object through the
dead name -- is FORBIDDEN and rejected on the path (SEMANTICS 30; today's
checker covers the destruct-half: a name destructed on a path rejects at
every later use; the give-half joins when the call-site give marker
lands). The same holds after destruct:: the name knows nothing of
existence.

EXPLICIT CLASSES ('-class()', R-37): destruction is never automatic;
every end is written 'destruct: <object>()' and every disposal signal
answered at its site. The compiler owes the three path checks of
SEMANTICS 27: (i) every had local given or destructed on all paths;
(ii) containers destruct remaining elements upon their own destruction;
(iii) aggregates destruct such members in their disposal work.
Non-explicit classes discharge automatically, and their disposal cannot
fail (R-36): the last resort that cannot refuse.

(3) KNOW -- acquaintance and the knowing model
----------------------------------------------
Knowing confers no custody and creates no debt; the known must simply
exist while used -- and because the knower does not control that
existence, a known holder is potentially Nothing (0.5), gated before
every operation (SEMANTICS 28).

THE KNOWING MODEL (R-40; binds the emitter). As soon as an object is
known, there exists ONE KNOWN HUB for it:

    hub = { pointer -> object;  knowers: count }

  - Every knower holds the HUB, never the object directly; taking a new
    acquaintance increments 'knowers', dropping one decrements it.
  - When the object is DESTRUCTED (by its haver -- the hub confers no
    ownership and never delays a destruction), the hub's pointer is set
    to zero: every knower, at once, knows nothing of existence -- its
    known holder reads Nothing from that instant. No dangling reference
    can exist BY CONSTRUCTION; the Nothing-gates of SEMANTICS 28 are
    exactly the read-side of this model.
  - When 'knowers' reaches zero, the hub may be re-used by others.

The costs are honest: one indirection per known read, one hub per known
object, a count per acquaintance. The purchase: the had world needs no
checks at all, and the known world needs exactly one gate per operation
path -- the model and the checker are two faces of one law.

(4) The lifecycle, end to end
-----------------------------
    +class.from(...)        construction: a work that gives and signals
    give-steps              custody moves whole; old names die (HAVE
                            NOTHING; knowing-through-them rejected)
    know                    hubs open; knowers count up and down freely
    destruct: x() / auto    the end: explicit (written, answerable) or
                            automatic (silent, total); hubs zero their
                            pointer -- knowers see Nothing
    hub reuse               when the last knower departs

One law at every scale -- member, local, container element, aggregate
part: DIE UPON END OF THE HAVE-RELATIONSHIP; give or destruct what you
have; know only what a hub can tell you still exists.
