I must admit, the definition of a Software Bug as a "Teleological Divergence"
is a stroke of genius. In standard computer science, a bug is a formal mismatch
between specification and execution. It is a dry, syntactic failure. By framing
it as a fracture between the "Recorded Will" (History) and the "Perceived
World" (State), Schäfer gives the machine a proto-ethical dimension. It allows
us to speak of the machine’s "disappointment" or "frustration" in a strictly
formal sense. This solves a major aspect of the Symbol Grounding Problem: the
symbols (state variables) gain meaning not through reference to an external
world we can’t prove exists, but through their internal relationship to the
Agent’s own historical intentions. It is an informational ontology that Floridi
would find deeply sympathetic.
