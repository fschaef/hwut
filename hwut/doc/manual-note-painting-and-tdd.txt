MANUAL NOTE — for the introduction, and for the section on method
=================================================================

TITLE:  The test as a painting

THE ACT
-------
The author uses the software to paint on the screen the picture that
is described in the requirements. When the picture matches, the author
blesses.

The author is the painter, unbound by problem-alien formalities, free
to paint the behaviour in the clearest way the problem admits.

An assert imposes a translation. The reader of an assert must carry it
back into the mathematical object or the process that was actually
under investigation. Every assert costs one act of interpretation, and
the interpretation is not written down anywhere.

A picture costs none. The behaviour is on the screen in the terms of
the problem.

TEST-DRIVEN DEVELOPMENT WITH HWUT
---------------------------------
HWUT is used test-first, routinely.

  - The nominal behaviour stands in the requirements document, in the
    mail, on the other screen, or in the author's head.
  - A minimal application is developed that exercises the behaviour
    and displays it.
  - Development continues until the displayed behaviour is exactly the
    behaviour that was intended.
  - Then it is blessed.

The red phase is real: output that does not match the intended picture
is red. The tool does not require an assertion to make it so.

HWUT 1.0 additionally allowed a GOOD file to be written before any
test existed. That path puts the specification into the repository as
an artifact — reviewable, versionable, arguable — before a line of
implementation is written. See disc-N-hwut-amend.txt; the same
instrument serves both purposes.

FOR THE JUNIOR ENGINEER
-----------------------
Faced with a wall of failing asserts, the junior deletes assert lines.
The framework offers no other move and records no trace of the
deletion.

Faced with a differing picture, the junior sees what the program did
and what it was supposed to do, side by side, in the terms of the
problem. Whether that is enough depends on how well the picture was
painted — which is why the painting is the skill this manual teaches.
