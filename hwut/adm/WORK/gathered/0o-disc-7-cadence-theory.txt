'test-wishlist.sh' -- is a suite that SPAWNS PROCESSES. What the
warning told the reader is 'this test runs subprocesses', fourteen

A WARNING THAT FIRES ON A PROPERTY OF THE TEST RATHER THAN ON ITS
INSTABILITY IS NOT EVIDENCE. It is a hunt: a reader who acts on it
changes tests that were never broken, and a reader who learns to
ignore it stops reading the one line that mattered.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS WRONG WITH 'rt = stddev(delta) / average(delta)'
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The measure is not wrong; it is UNCALIBRATED and MEASURES THE WRONG
CLOCK. Four faults, each independently sufficient.

f-1  IT IS WALL CLOCK.  'operations/run/core.py' marks with
     'time.monotonic()'. A line that WAITS ON A SUBPROCESS is timing
     the operating system's scheduler, not the test. Under load it
     varies hugely and honestly. THE TEST DID NOTHING DIFFERENT.

f-2  THERE IS NO NULL MODEL.  No one has measured what 'rt' looks
     like for a KNOWN-STEADY line on THIS machine under THIS load. A
     threshold without that is a number somebody liked. 'Unsteady'
     means 'more variable than steady', and nothing in the run says
     what steady is.

f-3  THE SAMPLE IS FAR TOO SMALL.  At n = 5 the standard error of a
     standard-deviation estimate is roughly 35% of the value. An 'rt'
     computed from five repeats cannot separate a doubled variance
     from noise. Any threshold at that n has a false-positive rate
     nobody has computed -- and fourteen of sixty-four is 22%, which
     is about what one would expect from thresholding noise.

f-4  THE LOAD IS COMMON AND UNSUBTRACTED.  The repeats of ALL keys run
     under the same machine at the same time. A slow repeat is slow
     for EVERYONE. That common factor inflates every key's 'rt'
     together, and is exactly the part that is not the test's.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
RULED (chat, 2026-08-27): OFF BY DEFAULT; THE CAUTIOUS TESTER ASKS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
TIMING IS A MESS AND WILL STAY ONE, and the framework cannot measure
its way out. It sets '--jobs', which is ITS OWN parallelism -- NOT the
machine's load. A hundred niced processes and ten busy ones are not
comparable, and nothing the framework records tells them apart. A
'processes now / processes then' ratio multiplies a number we know by
a number we do not.

SO: STABILITY DOES NOT CHECK TIMING UNLESS ASKED.

    default        the VERDICT detectors only. Two runs of one test
                   that disagree on a verdict disagree, full stop --
                   no statistics between the reading and the report.
    '--cadence'    the timing findings, for a tester who knows the
                   machine, chose the moment, and knows what the
                   number is worth. THEY are the calibration; the
                   framework has none.

CADENCE IS NEVER A SHOW STOPPER: it does not stain, and it does not
move the exit status, asked for or not.

SHOULD IT BE ASKED FOR, it speaks only of the GROSS: a deviation above
5 SECONDS -- far past any resolution question -- AND some 50x the
usual delta for that line. Not 2x, not 5x. Nothing clearing both bars
is noise, and nothing below them can be told from noise by any means
the framework has.

FOUND WHILE BUILDING IT: 'THE USUAL DELTA' MUST BE THE MEDIAN, NOT
THE AVERAGE. 'spread / average' CANNOT EXCEED THE REPEAT COUNT -- one
slow run among four is at most 4x, whatever its size, because the
outlier drags up the very average it is measured against. A 50x bar
against the average is therefore unreachable below fifty repeats, and
would have been silent for ever. Against the MEDIAN the same outlier
scores thousands, and one slow repeat does not move it -- which is
exactly what 'usually' means.

The same arithmetic says what shape the rule describes: a line that is
USUALLY instant and ONCE was not. An ALTERNATING slowness puts half
the samples either side of the median and collapses the factor to 2x
-- correctly, because a line that is slow half the time is not an
outlier, it is a bimodal test.

WHAT REMAINS OPEN is whether the option is worth its code. Nobody has
measured whether a cadence finding predicts a later verdict flip. Off
by default, it can at least do no harm while that stays unknown.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THEORY IT WOULD NEED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CADENCE IS FOR, stated first, because the measure follows from
it: a line whose duration varies across repeats MAY be a race, a
timeout-dependent path, or contention -- a thing that will LATER
become a verdict flip. Cadence is a LEADING INDICATOR of instability,
never instability itself. That is why it warns and does not stain,
and that part of the design is right.

A leading indicator earns its place only if a reading of 'unsteady'
raises the probability of a future flip. Nothing has ever measured
that. THE FIRST WORK IS NOT A THRESHOLD, IT IS THAT MEASUREMENT: keep
cadence findings and verdict flips over many runs and ask whether the
first predicts the second. If it does not, the detector should go, and
the honest answer to this discussion may be its removal.

Should it survive, three corrections, in order of how much they buy:

t-1  MEASURE CPU, NOT WALL CLOCK.  A line that waits on a subprocess
     costs no CPU; a line that spins, races or retries does. procsitter
     already reports CPU time. This alone would silence most of the
     fourteen for a reason, not by tuning.

     COST: the timing sidecar records a second number, and 'D-x' would
     have to say which one a cadence reads. Wall clock stays -- it is
     what a person feels -- but it is REPORTED, not JUDGED.

t-2  SUBTRACT THE COMMON MODE.  Per repeat, form a load proxy from all
     keys that ran in it; a key is unsteady only where its variation
     exceeds what the common factor explains. The framework already
     has the data: it runs every key over the same repeats. This is
     ordinary common-mode rejection and it is the difference between
     'the machine was busy' and 'this test is unsteady'.

t-3  CALIBRATE, AND SAY THE SAMPLE SIZE.  Run a known-steady control
     line in the same repeats; the threshold is a multiple of the
     control's own 'rt', not a constant. And refuse the finding below
     a repeat count at which it can mean anything -- if that count is
     20, then '--repeat=5' should say 'too few repeats to judge
     cadence' and judge only the verdicts. ABSENCE OF EVIDENCE, NAMED.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE STEPS, SHOULD IT SURVIVE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECOMMENDED, as the smallest honest step: the cadence finding is not
printed by default. '--cadence' asks for it, and the help says what it
is -- a leading indicator, uncalibrated. The verdict detectors
(VERDICT, BYTES, LENGTH) keep the terminal, and they are the ones that
have a theory: two runs of one test that disagree on a VERDICT
disagree, full stop, with no statistics in between.

This costs one flag and removes fourteen sentences that mean nothing,
without deleting work that may yet be made sound.

OPEN: whether to correct it (t-1..t-3) or to withdraw it pending the
predictive measurement. The measurement is the honest first move and
nobody has made it.
